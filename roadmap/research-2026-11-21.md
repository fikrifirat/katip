# Research check: the last 30 days (22 Oct to 21 Nov 2026)

Before changing the roadmap we checked our reading of the problem against what people said
in the last 30 days. This note says how the check was run, what it confirmed, what it
challenged, and what it could not see. The short version is in [ROADMAP.md](../ROADMAP.md#research-check).

## How it was run

- Tool: the [last30days](https://github.com/mvanhorn/last30days-skill) skill (v4.5.0), run by
  hand from its `SKILL.md` because plugins can't be installed in the cloud session we used.
- Free sources only, no paid keys: Hacker News, GitHub and Polymarket through the engine, plus
  web searches for news, bar guidance and regulator pages.
- **Reddit was not searched.** Reddit refused the network we ran from (HTTP 403). X, YouTube and
  TikTok need paid keys and were left out. Those are where lawyers talk most freely
  (r/Lawyertalk, legal X), so the lawyer's own voice is the biggest gap in this check.
- Three engine runs: (1) Harvey and legal AI tools, (2) masking personal data before LLMs and
  local AI for lawyers, (3) legal AI in the Netherlands and Turkey. Run 3 returned almost nothing
  relevant: Turkish and Dutch legal-tech talk does not happen on HN or GitHub. Web searches
  covered those markets instead.
- Many web results are vendor marketing or analyst summaries (Sacra, vendor blogs). They are
  marked as such below and used only for what the market says about itself.

## What it confirmed

**1. The code is not the moat.** Three "open-source Harvey" projects reached Hacker News in four
weeks ([CaseCite, 70 points](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50273623);
[sorryharvey.com](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50289163); a
[paid press release](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50368948) about another). The top
replies say the same thing: "someone else came out with an 'open source Harvey' (called 'Mike')
months ago and it hasn't really put a dent in uptake of Harvey or its main rival, Legora"
([arctic-true](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50273883)); "it's the industry itself that's
very tricky and old fashioned", with documents locked in document-management systems
([armcat](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50289485)); and, bluntly, "you don't have the
access to jurisprudence" ([postepowanieadm](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50273772)).
This supports the moat we wrote down on 20 Nov: lawyer-graded private data, access to case law,
distribution and trust. Technology gets us in the door; it does not keep us there.

**2. In Europe, where data is processed decides who gets bought.** The Dutch bar (NOvA)
[revised its AI recommendations in November 2026](https://www.advocatenorde.nl/nieuws/nova-start-campagne-over-verantwoord-ai-gebruik-en-de-herziene-aanbevelingen-ai-de)
([PDF](https://www.advocatenorde.nl/sites/default/files/2026-11/Aanbevelingen%20AI%20in%20de%20advocatuur%202026%20DEF.pdf)).
It asks firms to keep storage and processing of confidential client data inside the EEA as far
as possible, to decide in advance which tasks an AI agent may do on its own, and to be open with
clients about AI use. Analyst write-ups say residency is often the gate for a firm-wide rollout
in Europe ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/chat/h/a9637d18-abb1-4158-a0cf-23dab759c258/), analyst
summary). This supports the Dutch version, the local masking layer and the laptop / firm-server
tiers.

**3. Turkish guidance points the same way.** The Union of Turkish Bar Associations (TBB)
published AI guidance on 28 Aug 2026 that stresses client confidentiality, human oversight and
the lawyer's final responsibility (reported in Turkish law-firm bulletins; we did not read the
full text). KVKK has published notes on chatbots and on agentic AI
([Erdem & Erdem summary](https://www.erdem-erdem.av.tr/bilgi-bankasi/etken-yapay-zeka-agentic-ai-baslikli-kisisel-verileri-koruma-kurumu-dokumani-yayimlandi)).
Together with the KVKK guide for lawyers (22 Sep 2026) this backs masking as the trust layer and
the lawyer's grade button as a feature, not a nice-to-have.

**4. Fake citations keep costing lawyers.** A Delaware Chancery partner was fined $2,500 for
AI-hallucinated citations ([Bloomberg Government, 13 Nov 2026](https://news.bgov.com/ip-law/delaware-chancery-court-sanctions-attorney-for-ai-hallucinations));
courts now also sanction co-signers and local counsel
([ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/web/article/more-courts-are-coming-down-on-non-offending-counsel-for-ai-missteps));
one tracker had logged over 2,000 such court cases worldwide by September 2026 (as reported in
the same coverage). These are US cases; we found no Turkish sanction to cite and do not claim one.
It still makes a citation check before anything leaves the case worth moving up.

**5. Running models on a Mac is becoming normal.** "Apple has new Mac app coming to make running
local AI models easy" drew 580 points and 570 comments on HN
([9to5Mac, 10 Nov](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50446520)). This lowers the bar for the
laptop tier (Klasör, Kalkan) and for firms that want nothing to leave the office.

## What it challenged

**6. A consumer-facing "AI writes your petition" is politically hot in Turkey.** In April 2026,
78 bar associations, including Istanbul, Ankara and Izmir, jointly opposed the Justice Ministry's
plan for an AI platform that gives citizens sample petitions
([Cumhuriyet](https://www.cumhuriyet.com.tr/turkiye/78-barodan-ortak-aciklama-savunma-hakki-yapay-zekaya-devredilemez-2498459);
[OECD AI incident entry](https://oecd.ai/en/incidents/2026-04-25-b282)). TBB said the state's
first duty is to widen people's access to lawyers
([BirGün](https://www.birgun.net/haber/tbb-den-gurlek-e-yapay-zeka-yaniti-devletin-oncelikli-gorevi-yurttaslarin-avukata-erisimini-yayginlastirmaktir-708452)).
For *Cebindeki hukuk* this cuts both ways: an AI-only answer for the public would meet the same
wall, while "a lawyer reviews it at tariff fees" is close to what TBB says it wants. **Change:**
the marketplace keeps a lawyer in every answer, and nothing public-facing ships before a written
opinion on bar rules (Av.K. m.35, advertising, fee tariff).

**7. Research and drafting assistants are a crowded field, and the general chatbots are the main
rival.** New or growing Turkish players: Jupytr (says it serves Hergüner, Garanti BBVA and
Allianz, and names Claude and ChatGPT as its biggest rivals,
[Webrazzi, Jun 2026](https://webrazzi.com/2026/06/26/yerli-akilli-hukuk-asistani-jupytr/)),
Turbo Law ([Webrazzi, Oct 2026](https://webrazzi.com/2026/10/02/yapay-zeka-destekli-hukuk-girisimi-turbo-law/)),
Leagle, and Apilex, which is expanding to Germany, France, Spain and Italy. All claims are the
companies' own. Harvey and Legora launched rival "Intake" products on the same day
([MLex](https://www.mlex.com/pulse/articles/2537806)), and analysts report many firms using
both. **Change:** keep plugging into Claude and ChatGPT (Kalkan, the relay) instead of fighting
them, and lead the platform with what they can't do: masking on the device, verified Turkish
sources inside the case, and lawyer-graded answers.

**8. "Tech must win" needs a narrower meaning.** The HN threads above show a cheaper clone does
not move buyers by itself. The tech edge that matters for us is the one a cloud model can't copy:
masking on the device before anything leaves, small own models that make that cheap, and a
benchmark (KVKK-Bench) others measure against. **Change:** each roadmap item says which part of
the moat it builds.

## What it could not see

- What Turkish and Dutch lawyers say among themselves this month. Reddit was blocked and X was
  not available; Turkish legal talk mostly happens on X, WhatsApp groups and bar events, none of
  which this check reached. The pilot interviews in the roadmap are the way to fill this.
- Whether the Justice Ministry platform launched, and the full text of the TBB guide.
- Prices: we found no new public price points for Turkish legal AI tools since September.
